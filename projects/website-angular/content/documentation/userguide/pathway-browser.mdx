## The Pathway Browser

The Pathway Browser is the primary means of viewing and interacting with pathways in Reactome.

See our Youtube Video explaining [navigating the Pathway Browser](<https://youtu.be/zfdGmFnsvIU>) for more information. The Pathway Browser's Tour button, in its top right corner, plays the same walkthrough, recorded on the former Pathway Browser.

The Pathway Browser includes tools for analysing datasets and exploring pathways. These tools allow several types of analysis:

* Pathway over-representation analysis and pathway topology-based analysis
* Comparison of a pathway with its equivalent in another species
* The overlay of user-supplied expression data onto a pathway
* The overlay of protein-protein or protein-compound interaction data from external databases or user-supplied data onto a pathway

See the section [Reactome Analysis Tools](</documentation/userguide/analysis>) for details of the analysis tools.

The Pathway Browser is opened from the Pathway Browser button on the Homepage:

![The Pathway Browser button on the Homepage](/uploads/documentation/userguide/pathway-browser/home-pathway-browser-button.png)

When the Pathway Browser opens without a pathway, it shows a map of all Reactome pathways. Pathways are organized hierarchically and often have sub-pathways: in the map, each top-level pathway is a region, with its sub-pathways nested inside it, each sized by its molecular content. Click a region to select that pathway, double-click it to open its diagram, and right-click to move back up a level. This map is also used to display data analysis results. At intermediate levels, pathways are often represented as interactive illustrations, with selectable regions that act as links to the lower levels, which are represented by detailed pathway diagrams.

![The map of all pathways, which the Pathway Browser opens on](/uploads/documentation/userguide/pathway-browser/pathway-overview-map.png)

Detailed pathway diagrams use a style based on Systems Biology Graphical Notation. They have several features intended to show an appropriate level of detail, including subpathway shading and the fade-in of commonly occurring small molecules and molecular structures as you zoom in.

To see the details of a specific pathway, double-click it in the map or an illustration. Alternatively, click its name in the Event Hierarchy panel on the left.

Features of the Pathway Browser are numbered in the picture below.

![The Pathway Browser, with its features numbered](/uploads/documentation/userguide/pathway-browser/pathway-browser-labelled.png)

1. **Search** – searches the pathway on screen, or all pathways, for a protein, gene, small molecule, reaction or pathway. Results in the pathway on screen are highlighted in the diagram.
2. **Species** – Reactome is a database of curated human biological pathways. These human pathways are used to computationally infer equivalent pathways in model organisms (described in detail [here](</documentation/inferred-events>)). Use the Species menu to select a species and view the predicted pathway. Note that infectious disease pathways that show the interaction of pathogens with human proteins are included in Homo sapiens.
3. **Overlay** – overlays interactors on the diagram: protein-protein or protein-compound interactions from IntAct, other external databases, or your own data. See Molecular Interaction Overlay in [Reactome Analysis Tools](</documentation/userguide/analysis>).
4. **Event Hierarchy panel** – shows the hierarchical organisation of Reactome pathways and pathway events or steps, known as reactions. When the Pathway Browser is opened, only the top level of the hierarchy is shown, as an alphabetical list of Reactome’s main topics (superpathways). Most topics are split into sub-pathways, which may be further divided into sub-sub-pathways. Sub-pathways are revealed by clicking the arrow to the left of the pathway name, and hidden by clicking it again. Above the hierarchy is the path to the pathway on screen; the house button goes back to the map of all pathways. See [Event Hierarchy](#event-hierarchy) below.
5. **Analyze** – opens the analysis tools, where you can upload your own data. See [Reactome Analysis Tools](</documentation/userguide/analysis>).
6. **Compare** – for a disease pathway, or a normal pathway with disease variants, shows the normal and the disease pathway side by side.
7. **Title** – the name of the pathway on screen.
8. **Tour** – plays a video walkthrough of the Pathway Browser, recorded on the former version.
9. **Layout** – shows or hides the Event Hierarchy and Details panels, or expands the diagram to fill the window.
10. **Light and dark** – switches between a light and a dark colour scheme.
11. **Cite us** – how to cite Reactome, and the pathway on screen.
12. **Home** – the Reactome logo links to the Homepage. The number beside it is the Reactome release you are viewing.
13. **Zoom and move** – the slider zooms in and out, the arrows move the diagram, and the centre button fits the whole diagram to the panel. You can also drag the diagram, and use your mouse scroll wheel or trackpad to zoom.
14. **Thumbnail** – an overview of the whole diagram, with the region on screen marked. Drag in it to move around the diagram.
15. **Legend** – explains the shapes and lines used in diagrams. See [Pathway Diagrams](#pathway-diagrams) below.
16. **Details panel** – details of what is selected, in tabs: **Details** (a description, literature references and more), **Molecule** (the molecules involved), **Results** (the results of an analysis, when one is shown), **Expression** (tissue expression from the Gene Expression Atlas), **Info** (help on the view on screen) and **Download** (the diagram and its data in several formats). The content depends on the type of object selected, i.e. pathway, reaction, complex, set, protein or small molecule (see [The Details panel](</documentation/userguide/details-panel>)).

## <a id="event-hierarchy">Event Hierarchy</a>

The order of reactions from top to bottom in the Event Hierarchy panel usually follows their order as steps in the pathway so that the preceding reaction is above and the subsequent reaction below, but this is not always the case. Pathways may not be linear; they can be circular or branched. Consequently, reactions often have multiple preceding or subsequent reactions. In the diagram, arrows lead from a reaction's outputs to the reactions that follow.

The Event Hierarchy panel provides a nested structure containing large topics that are organised as pathways and subpathways and can be expanded to show individual events and steps. The icons next to the events describe them.

You can hover over the icon in the Event Hierarchy panel to show a tool-tip that describes the event, as illustrated below.

## <a id="pathway-diagrams">**Pathway Diagrams**</a>

Pathway Diagrams represent pathways as a series of connected molecular events, known in Reactome as ‘reactions’, which can be considered as steps in the pathway.

Cellular compartments are represented as boxes with a double boundary. A typical diagram has a box to represent the cytosol, bounded by a double line that represents the plasma membrane. The area outside this box represents the extracellular space. Other organelles are represented as additional labelled boxes within the cytosol.

Molecules are placed in the physiologically-correct cellular compartment, or lie on the boundary of a compartment to indicate that they are in the corresponding membrane, e.g. a plasma membrane protein will be placed on the boundary of the cytosol.

The **Legend**, on the right of the diagram, shows each kind of molecule, reaction and connection:

![The diagram legend: molecules, reaction types and reaction attributes](/uploads/documentation/userguide/pathway-browser/legend-reaction-nodes.png)

Reactions typically include:

* Input and output molecules and, when relevant, a catalyst (see the first picture below).
* Each kind of molecule has its own shape, as the Legend shows: proteins, genes, RNA, small molecules, complexes, sets, cells and polymers. Drugs are marked with an ℞ symbol.
* Sets, i.e. proteins or small molecules that are functionally equivalent, have curly-bracket sides.
* Subpathways and interacting pathways are shown as boxes with a pathway icon; see [Enhanced high-level diagrams](#ehld) below.
* Reaction input and output molecules are joined by lines to a central ‘reaction node’. Clicking this node selects the reaction.
* The outputs of a reaction have an arrowhead on the line connecting them to the reaction node.
* Reaction inputs/outputs are often connected by arrows to preceding or subsequent reactions (i.e. the preceding/subsequent steps in the pathway).
* Catalysts are connected to the reaction node by a line ending in a circle.
* Numbered boxes on the line between an input/output and the reaction node indicate the number of molecules of this type in the reaction (when n > 1).
* Molecules that regulate a reaction are connected to the reaction node by a line ending in an open triangle for positive regulation, or a bar for negative regulation (see the second picture below).
* Proteins or small molecules that are also part of a displayed set may be connected to the set by a dashed line.
* Reactions that represent a disease process, and objects associated with a disease, are drawn in red.

## <a id="ehld">**Enhanced high-level diagrams and Subpathway icons**</a>

Many pathways at the higher levels of the hierarchy are too large to represent as a single, detailed pathway diagram. Instead they are often represented as an illustration, known as an enhanced high-level diagram, which graphically represents the subpathways as selectable regions of an illustration. When the mouse pointer is moved over a region, it is highlighted. When you click a region it becomes selected, the corresponding subpathway is selected in the Event Hierarchy panel, and the Details panel updates to show details of the subpathway. Double-clicking a region opens the corresponding detailed Pathway Diagram.

In the Event Hierarchy panel, pathways that have an enhanced high-level diagram have their own icon (see the table above); hover over it to see "Pathway with an enhanced diagram".

![An enhanced high-level diagram: Hemostasis](/uploads/documentation/userguide/pathway-browser/enhanced-high-level-diagram.png)

Within a pathway diagram, other pathways are shown as boxes with a pathway icon. Selecting one has the same result as selecting a region of an enhanced high-level diagram as described above. The Legend distinguishes **subpathways**, which are part of the pathway on screen, from **interacting pathways**, which are elsewhere in the hierarchy but connected to this one.

### **Subpathway Shading**

When a Pathway Diagram contains subpathways, the reactions of each subpathway are highlighted with a band of colour of its own and labelled with the subpathway name, to help locate it in the larger diagram. Below is the diagram for Signaling by ERBB4. Its four subpathways are shown in four colours.

![Signaling by ERBB4, with its four subpathways coloured](/uploads/documentation/userguide/pathway-browser/subpathway-shading.png)

## <a id="pathway-diagram-zoom">**Pathway Diagram zoom detail level**</a>

The pathway diagram shows different levels of detail, depending on the zoom level. When zoomed out, as above, subpathways are coloured and labelled, and trivial molecules, such as water and ATP, are not shown. As you zoom in, the subpathway colouring fades away and the trivial molecules fade into view; when interactors are shown, the badges marking proteins that have known interactors appear too:

![Zoomed in: subpathway colouring gone, small molecules shown](/uploads/documentation/userguide/pathway-browser/zoom-2-small-molecules.png)

Zoomed in further, proteins and small molecules show their structures, if available:

![Zoomed in further: molecular structures in the boxes](/uploads/documentation/userguide/pathway-browser/zoom-3-structures.png)

## <a id="navigating-pathway-diagrams">**Navigating Pathway Diagrams**</a>

The Pathway Diagram, Event Hierarchy and Details panels are interactively connected. Selecting an entity (molecular object) or event in the diagram reveals relevant information in the Details panel. Clicking a pathway name in the Event Hierarchy opens the corresponding Pathway Diagram, and reveals details of the pathway in the Details panel. If a subpathway is contained within the diagram, clicking it in the Event Hierarchy selects it in the diagram. Hovering over an event in the Event Hierarchy highlights it in the diagram, fading out the rest, as in the picture below, where the pointer is over ‘Platelet calcium homeostasis’.

![Hovering over a subpathway in the Event Hierarchy highlights it in the diagram](/uploads/documentation/userguide/pathway-browser/subpathway-selection.png)

Similarly, selecting a reaction in the Event Hierarchy highlights that reaction in the pathway diagram and updates the Details panel to show details of the reaction. The view re-centres and zooms to show it.

### Event Hierarchy **Exercises**

This exercise is to check that you understand the organisation of the Event Hierarchy panel. You don’t need to look at the diagram.

From the Home page, search for PDGF signaling.

In the results page, open the pathway's page and expand its locations in the Pathway Browser. **DON’T** click on a location before expanding the hierarchy! This will open the pathway diagram in the Pathway Browser.

Look at the Event Hierarchy panel on the left.

1. How many sub-pathways does this pathway have?
2. How many reactions are in the first sub-pathway?
3. What reaction(s) follow ‘Translocation of PDGF from ER to Golgi’? *Hint: select it, and follow the arrows from its outputs in the diagram. If the Details panel hides too much of the diagram, use the Layout button in the top right corner to hide it.*

3. What happens if you click in the Event Hierarchy on the reaction ‘Binding of ATP to P2X receptors’?

**More Information**

There are 5 subtypes of reaction node, indicating reaction subclasses. They are shown under Reaction Types in the Legend (see [Pathway Diagrams](#pathway-diagrams) above).

* Open squares represent ‘transition’, i.e. a change of state that is not one of the defined subclasses.
* Solid circles represent ‘association’, i.e. binding.
* Double-bordered circles represent ‘dissociation’.
* A square with two slashes represents an ‘omitted process’. This is used when the full details of a reaction have been deliberately omitted. This is most commonly used for events that include representative members of a large family to illustrate the general behaviour of the group. It can be used for reactions that occur with no fixed order or stoichiometry, or for degradation events where the output is a random set of fragments.
* Squares containing a question mark represent an ‘uncertain process’, where some details of the reaction are known, but the process is thought to be more complex than represented. Explanatory details are typically included in the Description.

### Reaction Node **Exercises**

This exercise is to check that you understand reaction node subtypes.

From the Home page, search for the pathway ‘Effects of PIP2 hydrolysis’ and open it in the Pathway Browser (in any of the several locations).

1. What symbol represents the reaction for ‘Binding of IP3 to the IP3 receptor’?
2. What symbol represents the reaction ‘IP3R tetramer:I(1,4,5)P3:4xCa2+ transports Ca2+ from platelet dense tubular system to cytosol’? What subtype of reaction is this?
3. Open the subpathway ‘Arachidonate production from DAG’. What is the name of the catalyst for ‘2-AG hydrolysis to arachidonate by MAGL’? Can you name the three outputs of this reaction?
4. Can you find the UniProt ID for the catalyst? Hint: There are several ways to find this, two require you to select something!
