## Cortical development dynamics across autism spectrum disorder mouse models

In their June 2026 Nature research article, “[Cortical development dynamics across autism spectrum disorder mouse models](<https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10679-1>)”, Schwarz et al. report convergence in brain cortex gene expression perturbations across monogenic mouse models of autism spectrum disorder (ASD).

A compendium of [Reactome-derived protein-protein interactions (PPIs)](</about/news/79-new-protein-protein-interaction-files>) was one of the resources used to construct a reference PPI network that was then used for [network colocalization analysis](<https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1257601>) of differentially expressed genes identified by single-nucleus multi-omic sequencing of brain samples from eleven monogenic mouse ASD models across three developmental stages. In total, 251 brain samples from both sexes and two brain regions were used. The authors find that mutations linked to ASD result in transient developmental delay of the brain cortex. ASD-linked mutations converge on perturbations of the radial glial cell lineage. The largest transcriptional differences are seen in neurons at early postnatal stages. The network colocalization analysis shows molecular convergence across models within each developmental stage. Female mice often display larger magnitudes in gene expression changes than male mice. Significant downregulation of expression is observed in genes involved in [synaptic transmission](</PathwayBrowser/#/R-HSA-112315&PATH=R-HSA-112316>) and [ion channel transport](</PathwayBrowser/#/R-HSA-983712>).

The study highlights how, in spite of functional diversity in ASD, with more than 100 causal genes identified, transcriptional profiling demonstrates phenotypic convergence across mouse ASD models and reveals common neurobiological processes that are perturbed in ASD.
