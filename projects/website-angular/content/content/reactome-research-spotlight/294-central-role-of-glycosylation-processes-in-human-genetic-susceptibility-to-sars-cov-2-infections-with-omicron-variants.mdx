## Central role of glycosylation processes in human genetic susceptibility to SARS-CoV-2 infections with Omicron variants

In their February 2026 Nature Genetics paper “[Central role of glycosylation processes in human genetic susceptibility to SARS-CoV-2 infections with Omicron variants](<https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02484-9#Sec2>)”, Geller et al. describe a genome-wide association study of northern Europeans populations, that identified 13 genome-wide significant SNP loci correlated with susceptibility to infection with the Omicron strains of SARS-CoV-2. Reactome gene set enrichment analysis showed these 13 loci are in genes overrepresented in [protein glycosylation pathways](</PathwayBrowser/#/R-HSA-5173105&PATH=R-HSA-392499,R-HSA-597592>). The SNP variant most strongly correlated with increased susceptibility to infection with Omicron strains of SARS-CoV-2 is within the ST6GAL1 gene, encoding an enzyme that catalyzes addition of terminal alpha 2,6-sialic acids to galactose-containing N-linked glycans. Human proteins with this post-translational modifications include ones recognized by infecting viral particles. And the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, heavily glycosylated in all SARS-CoV-2 strains, shows reduced sialylation levels in Omicron virus particles, which may promote stronger binding to host cell ACE2 receptors, co-opted as viral binding sites on the cell surface, and suggesting a plausible mechanism for the increased human infectivity of Omicron viruses.
