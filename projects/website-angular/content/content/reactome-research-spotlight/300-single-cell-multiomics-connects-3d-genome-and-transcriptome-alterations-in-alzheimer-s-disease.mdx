## Single-cell multiomics connects 3D genome and transcriptome alterations in Alzheimer’s disease

Alzheimer's Disease (AD), the devastating neurodegenerative condition that is the most common cause of dementia, is known to be accompanied by pathological accumulations of proteins (extracellular amyloid plaques and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles), as well as significantly altered transcription patterns in various cell types in the brain. The causes of the changes in gene expression are not well understood. [Zhang et al.](<https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adz1652>) (2026) in the July 23 issue of Science have tested the possibility that changes in the 3-dimensional configuration of chromatin in nuclei of various cell types in the prefrontal cortices of individuals with AD may underlie the pathology-associated changes in gene expression.

Using a newly developed technique, Genome Architecture and Gene Expression by sequencing (GAGE-seq), and more established techniques such as transposase accessibility (snATAC-seq) and Xenium tissue-based transcript sequencing, they find that mingling of active and inactive chromatin (A/B mingling) is increased, 3-dimensional proximity of short-range cis-regulatory elements (1-128 kb) to transcription start sites (TSSs) is decreased, and 3-dimensional proximity of mid-range cis-regulatory elements (128-4096 kb) to TSSs is increased in individuals with AD. These changes appear to be the result of altered chromatin looping and result in increased expression of senescence-associated genes in glial cells, dysregulation of X-linked genes in females, and changes to expression of many other genes.

To characterize the possible physiological effects of the altered chromatin and transcription, Zhang et al. map the affected genes onto genesets from Reactome pathways and pathways from other databases, and correlate the degree of transcription alteration with the degree of chromatin A/B mingling. The results show that the chromatin alterations appear to result in strong transcriptional upregulation of mitochondrial function, especially [respiratory electron transport](</PathwayBrowser/#/R-HSA-611105>), while [transmission across chemical synapses](</PathwayBrowser/#/R-HSA-112315>) and [signaling by receptor tyrosine kinases](</PathwayBrowser/#/R-HSA-9006934>) are strongly downregulated.

The discovery of the pathways affected by chromatin and transcription may guide the development of therapeutics for AD. For example, drugs that target metabolic and oxidative stress are currently under-represented in clinical trials and may be prioritized.
