##  Identification and targeting of regulators of SARS-CoV-2–host interactions in the airway epithelium 

In the May 2025 issue of Science Advances, [Dirvin et al](<https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adu2079?url_ver=Z39.88-2003&rfr_id=ori:rid:crossref.org&rfr_dat=cr_pub%20%200pubmed>). used single-cell transcriptomics and network-based algorithms on primary human airway cells to identify the key master regulator proteins hijacked by SARS-CoV-2, and then performed a large-scale screen to find drugs capable of reversing these effects. They used Reactome pathway analysis to characterize the biological processes, including [membrane trafficking](<https://reactome.org/PathwayBrowser/#/R-HSA-199991>) and [ infectious disease pathways](<https://reactome.org/PathwayBrowser/#/R-HSA-5663205>), that were modulated by the eleven most promising drug candidates.
